The Meaning

A Night Woven From an Old Longing

"Tu Kuja, Man Kuja" — Persian for "Where are You, and where am I" — is one of the most cherished refrains in Sufi devotional poetry: a seeker's whisper across the distance between the self and the Divine.

This year, EduThanzeel brings that longing into a single evening of ishal — soulful vocal remembrance, gentle recitation and shared reflection — held not for the public, but for our own extended family: every staff member, and the people who make their work possible at home.

Come as you are. Bring your parents, your children, your quiet evening. There is nothing to prepare — only to arrive.

The Evening

What the Night Holds

A simple, unhurried program — built around presence rather than performance.

Live Ishal

Devotional recitation and vocal remembrance performed live through the evening.

A Family Gathering

Staff, spouses, parents and children — seated together, not separated by role.

Light Refreshments

Tea, dates and a warm meal shared after the recitation concludes.

A Quiet Reflection

A short closing word tying the evening back to EduThanzeel's own purpose.
